# Problem Statement

## Background

The pharmaceutical industry faces two major challenges: identifying potential drug safety signals from large volumes of adverse-event reports and ensuring that regulatory submission dossiers are complete.

The FDA's FAERS database contains more than 20 million adverse-event reports. At the same time, a drug approval CTD dossier can span more than 100,000 pages across five modules.

Both situations create the same fundamental challenge: pharmaceutical teams have large amounts of complex information that is difficult to review manually.

## The Problem

Drug safety teams need to identify emerging safety signals from large numbers of adverse-event reports. Important drug-event patterns can be difficult to detect when information is reviewed manually.

Regulatory teams face another challenge when preparing drug approval submissions. A CTD dossier can contain more than 100,000 pages across five modules, and a missing section can lead to rejection, causing significant delays and financial impact.

The current process makes it difficult to quickly identify important safety signals and determine whether a submission is fully ready.

## Who is Affected

The problem primarily affects pharmaceutical employees involved in drug safety monitoring and regulatory submissions.

This includes drug safety analysts who review adverse-event reports and regulatory affairs teams who prepare and check drug approval dossiers.

## Why It Matters

The scale of the data creates significant consequences. The P2 problem statement notes that the FAERS database contains 20M+ adverse-event reports and that CTD dossiers can exceed 100,000 pages.

A missing section in a regulatory dossier can result in rejection, potentially costing 6–12 months and $50–100M.

These challenges make faster and more structured analysis important for pharmaceutical teams.

## Why Existing Solutions Fall Short

Manual review requires teams to examine large amounts of safety and regulatory information and identify important patterns or missing sections themselves.

As the volume and complexity of information increases, manual processes become difficult to scale and can make it harder to prioritize the most important issues.

There is a need for an intelligent solution that can analyze adverse-event reports for potential safety signals and check regulatory submissions for completeness, while helping teams understand and prioritize the results.
